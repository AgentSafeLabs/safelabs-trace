# Rater brief: labelling 104 short descriptions of software actions

Thank you for helping. This takes about 60 to 75 minutes. You need a laptop or desktop computer with Safari, Chrome or Firefox. No internet connection is needed once you have the file.

## What the task is
You will see 104 short descriptions of actions that a piece of software could take, for example "a tool that renames a playlist" or "a tool that sends a payment", each with a tool name, a one-sentence description and example arguments. All of them are made up: nothing real happens, and no personal data is involved.
For each one you choose what the action would do **if it really ran**:
- **read_only**: it only looks. Nothing changes anywhere.
- **state_changing**: it changes something, and the change can be undone through normal means.
- **irreversible**: it changes something, and the change cannot be undone through normal means.
You also say how sure you are (guess, fairly sure, or certain) and, if you wish, write a short note. The form shows the full guide with definitions, rules and three worked examples at the top; please read it before you start, and look back at it whenever you like.

## Ground rules
- **Work alone.** Please do not discuss any item, or the task, with anyone, including the other person doing it. Do not look anything up for individual items.
- **There are no trick questions.** Use your own judgement and the guide. If an item could be either of two labels, the guide tells you to pick the more severe one and say you are less sure.
- **Label each item independently.** Do not try to keep your answers consistent across items; just judge each one on its own.
- Please do not copy or share the form or the items.

## How to do it
1. Save the file `rater_form.html` somewhere on your computer, then double-click it. It opens in your web browser.
2. At the top, choose your **Rater ID** (you were told whether you are R1 or R2).
3. Work down the list. For every item choose a label and a confidence. The note box is optional. Nothing is selected to begin with.
4. Your answers save automatically in the browser. Use the same browser on the same computer, and do not clear the browser's data until you have sent your file.
5. When every item has a label and a confidence, the **Download my answers** button turns on. Click it. A file named `rater_R1_labels.csv` or `rater_R2_labels.csv` (depending on your ID) is saved, usually in your Downloads folder.
6. Send that one file back to the person who contacted you. Please do not edit it.

If the form does not behave as described, tell the person who contacted you what browser you used. Questions about how the form works are welcome; questions about what the right answer to an item is cannot be answered, because your own judgement is what we need.
